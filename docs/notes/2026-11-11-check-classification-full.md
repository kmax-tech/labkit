# Classifying the checks per cause — the full pass, 2026-11-11

*Every gate finding labkit tells, by function and cause, in the four dimensions of [the sample](2026-11-11-check-classification-sample.md): 216 finding sites of the inventory (`python -m labkit.tools.check_inventory`) as 262 causes. Basis, procedure, outcome and effect are read from the source by the inventory; the subject and the causes of the 25 sites that receive their rule are one reader's reading at the code, not checked independently. A first full draft, not a checked stock. No new round on the reasons: where a basis is open it stays open.*

*Revised 2026-11-12, twice: two independent readers assigned a sample of 41 causes (section "A second reading"), and the table carries what they agreed on. Before that the seven points under "What does not fit" were worked through. Three were faults in the code and are fixed there, the subjects got a definition each and were applied again, and the table and the counts below show the state after that.*

## What was added to the inventory's columns

The inventory gives, per site: the basis (`GateFinding.rule`), the procedure (`evidence_kind`: exact or a static hint), the outcome (`GateFinding.outcome`) and the effect (`grade`). Two things were missing for a table per cause.

**The subject per cause.** The sample named seven values. They do not hold for the whole: eleven are needed, and one of the seven has no gate finding. A subject says what the finding calls wrong, and against what it is held. It belongs to the cause, not to the function: one function can tell causes of several subjects (`preflight._owed`, `code_check.promise_block_findings`, `run_record_check.provenance_findings`), so the table has no subject per function.

| Subject                             | Causes | The finding says …                                                                                                                                       |
|-------------------------------------|--------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| protocol text or head               | 83     | the protocol file is wrong as it stands: a field, a table of the head, the promise block's form — or it differs from what its first measurement recorded |
| record against the state            | 31     | an audit, review or run record does not cover, or no longer matches, the code, the files or the area as they are now                                     |
| run record in itself                | 26     | what a run recorded is wrong or missing: its kind, lineage, draws, sources, outputs, models                                                              |
| generated line or block             | 23     | a line or block labkit writes into the protocol or the report is missing or differs from its source                                                      |
| run record against the registration | 20     | the run's keys, constants, comparison, kept number or promise block differ from what the protocol registers                                              |
| records among themselves            | 20     | audit findings, their decisions, follow-ups and reviews do not fit each other, or one of them does not read                                              |
| repository or installation state    | 15     | the working tree, tracked files, hooks or git's objects are not in the state a check needs                                                               |
| code against the registration       | 9      | the area's code lacks what the protocol registers: a key literal, a constant, the prompt                                                                 |
| text beside the protocol            | 13     | a note's meta line or the area README's route is wrong                                                                                                   |
| report prose                        | 10     | a run id or a number in the report's own sentences has no source                                                                                         |
| area tests                          | 7      | the tests are missing, did not run or failed; a parser no test calls                                                                                     |
| content                             | 0      | the meaning is wrong — the agent audit's finding and the report review's claim, which are not gate findings                                              |

The column sums to 257: five causes are not assigned — the four of `import_check` (point 2 below) and `audit.early_for_tier` (point 7). Two rules decide where two subjects fit. A file that does not read belongs to the subject of that file, not of the check that wanted to read it: a head that does not read is "protocol text or head" also when report-check found it. A comparison of two things belongs to the one the finding calls wrong: a registration changed after its first measurement is "protocol text or head", since the record of the first measurement is the fixed side.

**The causes of a site that receives its rule.** 25 sites record no rule of their own: the cause passes it in. Each was followed to the place that decides the rule, and the site stands in the table once per cause. Two of them are one site told twice (`preflight._owed`, as a warning and, once frozen, as a blocker). One function may stand under two rules by its caller, and that is two causes: `preflight.tell_test_run` tells failed tests under LAB-016 where preflight holds the resolution check before the freeze, and under CODE-003 where finalize holds the area's tests.

## Counts

262 causes. Basis: 119 a known rule, 108 a technical contract, 35 an open assignment. Outcome: 201 violated, 32 deviation, 17 suspected, 6 not executable, 6 decided by the error at run time (not executable where the access is refused, else violated). Effect: 190 BLOCKER, 62 WARNING, 8 a warning that blocks once the protocol is frozen, 2 the grade of the audit finding they are about. The counts are causes as this table cuts them, not sites and not how often a finding occurs: a cause that groups several conditions of one rule (the promise block's form) counts once.

Outcome and effect are not always one of the four values. At sixteen causes they depend on the state when the check runs: six outcomes are decided by the error (an access refused is a check not executed), eight effects by `frozen`, two by the grade of the audit finding. The table says so in words in those cells.

## What did not fit, and what became of it

The first pass listed seven points. Their state on 2026-11-12:

1. **The subject belongs to the cause, not to the function.** Settled as the rule above; the table was cut per cause from the start.
2. **"Code against the registration" was too narrow for the stamp's coverage** (`import_check`, four causes). Whether the code does what the protocol registers and whether the audit covers the code are two questions, so the four causes left this subject. They were tried under "record against the state", which holds the other causes of audit coverage (the code changed after the audit, no analysis audit covers the area). The second reading does not carry that: one reader took it with doubt, the other answered `none`, and both wrote that no subject names the code's own reach. The four stand as not assigned. Whether audit coverage becomes a subject of its own is the author's decision.
3. **One function, two bases by caller** (`preflight.tell_test_run`). No fault: two gates hold the tests to two duties. The table has one row per caller.
4. **A site's rule overrode the cause's — a fault, fixed.** A declared scope that cannot be hashed was raised without a rule, so the error carried the default, an open assignment, and code-check's finding set the technical contract itself. The five causes in `promises.scope_sources` and `_function_source` set the technical contract now, and the finding passes the error's rule on. The same fault stood one step further: the field report told every promise block that does not read as a technical contract (`protocol_check.MustGap`), while code-check told the same cause under the rule its error names. The gap passes the rule on now, so `preflight.check_protocol_fields` and `report_check.freeze_gaps` have three rows each, as code-check has. What was not followed is listed below.
5. **"Variable" in the inventory was not always several causes — fixed.** `import_check._import_findings` took its rule from its only caller. The rule is the module's constant now, and the inventory reads the site as LAB-019.
6. **Outcome and effect depend on the state at sixteen causes.** A property of the checks, said under "Counts"; the four values stay.
7. **Subjects this reader was unsure of.** Applied again with the definitions and the two rules. Eight causes moved: the three of `registration_hold.registration_changes`, the promise block that does not read in `preflight._owed`, a protocol head that does not read in `report_blocks.block_gaps` and `report_hold.undeclared_findings` (all six to "protocol text or head"), and a run record that does not read in `answering_run` and `reference.comparison_gaps` (to "run record in itself"). One cause fits none of the eleven: `audit.early_for_tier` warns that an audit comes before the cheap first run. It calls no file wrong; it is about the order of steps. It stays not assigned: one case is no reason for a twelfth subject and none to force it into one. Both second readers answered `none` for it too.

What became of the four points left open on 2026-11-12:

- **`PromiseError` without a rule — closed.** The error has no default any more. The six causes of the block's parsing say that they are open. The five of `load`, `constants` and `with_constant` were followed to their output: only a lab's own script calls them, no gate catches the error, it ends the script. They record the technical contract.
- **The same cause told twice in one preflight — closed.** A promise block that does not read is told once, by the field report, with the repair of the block as its way out; code-check's other findings stand.
- **Two sites that tell a promise error under a rule of their own stay as they are**, since they tell another fact than the block's form: `audit_gaps` (an audit record cannot be held against a block that does not read — the technical contract) and `promises.scope_drift` (a recorded scope that cannot be read now counts as changed since the run — LAB-041).

## What "exact" covers

"Exact" names the condition a check tests: it is silent on a correct input, and what it tells is true of that condition. It does not promise that every breach of the rule is found. Limits that are written down, each at its source:

| Check                                               | Its condition                                                                        | A breach it does not find                                                                 | Source                                                         |
|-----------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| key literal of a registered id (`code_check`)       | a quoted string or keyword argument in the area's scripts and their imported helpers | a literal only in an imported helper, only under `analysis/`, or only in a comment        | the sample note, "Condition and rule sentence side by side"    |
| kept number (`run_record_check.kept_number_gaps`)   | `metrics.json` holds the registered key as a finite number                           | a wrong computation stored under the right key                                            | `docs/promise-block.md`, "What it does not prove"              |
| declared scope (`finalize.check_declared_scopes`)   | the declared region is byte-identical to the run's                                   | a change in code the scope calls and does not declare                                     | `docs/promise-block.md`, "What the declared-scope hash states" |
| registered constants (`intent_check`, `runlog`)     | every registered constant is recorded unchanged under its name                       | two differently named constants for one threshold; a value recorded and used wrongly      | `docs/promise-block.md`, "Constants: the name is the mapping"  |
| registered comparison (`reference.comparison_gaps`) | the reserved `ref_*` keys against the head's `[reference]`                           | a comparison computed under another name, and whether a changed one is marked exploratory | LAB-035, paragraph "A computation's comparison"                |
| result block (`report_blocks.block_gaps`)           | the declared run and equality of the block's body                                    | what the body says                                                                        | the function's docstring                                       |

The other exact checks were not examined for limits. No limit written down means "not examined", never "none".
